Red Hill Forest is the primary woodland region of Leonida's interior. Dense subtropical forest with the canopy cover and understory vegetation that real Florida woodland produces. **The Florida panther has its confirmed primary habitat here.**

The panther's presence defines the forest as much as the trees. An animal that is critically endangered in the real world, present but rare in Leonida, using the forest as the territory it retreats to when the coastal zones become too populated. This is where you'll find one, if you find one at all.

The woodland creates a specific set of conditions unlike anywhere else in Leonida. The canopy limits aerial observation. The terrain limits high-speed vehicle movement to the perimeter roads. The interior is foot and trail territory, which makes it useful for movement that does not want to be tracked by road.
%%%GROUND%%%
The canopy cover is the primary tactical feature of Red Hill Forest. **Aerial units have limited observation capability through dense forest canopy.** Ground units on the perimeter roads cannot enter the interior without transitioning to trail-capable movement.

The Florida panther presence is a practical consideration in the forest interior. Rare encounters rather than frequent ones, but the animal is territorial and the forest is its territory. Repeat encounters in the same spot are rarer still than the first one.

Trail access into the forest interior is possible but requires awareness of the panther territory and the limitations that dense vegetation places on movement speed and situational awareness. The forest is not hostile terrain in the military sense. It is simply terrain that rewards caution.
