How It Works

An artist who signs as Ahol Sniffs Glue has been spray-painting Vice City's junk. Dumped mattresses, dead fridges, traffic cones, pallets, busted TVs: if somebody left it on the curb, Ahol got to it first. The series is called Geographies of Trash, and there are 112 pieces of it.

You collect them with WAiNK. Get close, wait for the buzz, raise the phone and scan the painted object. Once it is in your collection, Vice City does the rest: people show up and haul the piece off as art. Each one is named after what it is and a number, like Mattress 72 or Cone 12.

None of it counts toward 100% or toward the 100 WAiNK scans. This one is for the completionists and anyone who likes walking alleys. Start in Vice Beach, where Ahol did a third of the work.

Tips

  • Walk the alleys. Most pieces sit by bins, fuse boxes and building sites, not on the main drag.
  • Start in Vice Beach and Washington Beach. About a third of the set is there, mostly in the back alleys between Indigo St, Grape St, Fig St and Thompson Ct.
  • Del Rio Blvd in La Perle and North Vice Ave in Downtown are the next best runs.
  • When WAiNK buzzes, raise the phone and look for the starburst on the junk itself, then scan.
  • These do nothing for the 100 WAiNK scans or for 100%. Do them for the collection, not the checklist.

Missables

  • None. The pieces stay on the street, and the collection can be finished in free roam at any time.

Frequently Asked

How many Ahol Street Art pieces are in GTA 6?
112, in 11 kinds: 10 mattresses, 8 tires, 9 wheels, 9 fridges, 7 armchairs, 8 TVs, 15 containers, 13 cones, 11 buckets, 13 pallets and 9 chairs.
Where is the Ahol street art in GTA 6?
Only in Vice City and on Vice Beach. Vice Beach has the most, then La Perle and Downtown. Check alleys, bin corners and building sites.
Does Ahol Street Art count toward 100% in GTA 6?
No. It has its own collection, and it does not count toward the 100 WAiNK scans either.
How do you collect Ahol Street Art in GTA 6?
Scan each piece with the WAiNK app on your phone. Once it is scanned, locals turn up and carry it away as art.
